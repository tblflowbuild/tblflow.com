A process map quickly gives you a number: "this step costs this much per month". The catch is that this number rests on durations and volumes that are **estimated**. It steers the discussion, it doesn't settle it.

## Measure the steps that matter

The process map now lets you enter **measurements**, step by step: actual minutes, volume, error rate and the **source of the measurement**. The "Estimated vs actual" panel shows:

- the **share of measured steps**, so you know how much confidence the total deserves;
- the estimate, the measurement and the gap, **compared on the measured steps only**, so you never mix apples and oranges;
- a reading per process and per step, with a link to the step in the canvas.

You don't need to measure everything: start with the steps that weigh the most.

## Before / after: prove a gain

Automating a step? **Freeze the map** first: TblFlow creates a copy named with the date. Once the change is made, open "Before / after" and compare the **total cost for the month** and the **number of steps** between the copy and the current map. The gain you announce rests on two states of the same map, not on a memory.

## What it doesn't do

Costs remain entries made by your team. TblFlow compares and formats them, it doesn't measure them for you. And amounts are only visible to people who have the right to read the cost fields.
